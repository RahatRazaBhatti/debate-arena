AUTHOR/ORGANIZATION: Stanford HAI, AI Index Steering Committee

TOPIC: AI coding capability trends, research comparing AI-assisted and traditional development, the future of software engineering roles

CONTENT:
Stanford's AI Index Report tracks AI capability on standardized
benchmarks over time. On SWE-bench, a benchmark built from real-world
GitHub issues that tests whether an AI system can understand a codebase,
diagnose a bug, and produce a working fix, model performance rose from
solving only 4.4% of problems in 2023 to 71.7% in 2024 -- a 67.3
percentage-point jump in a single year, among the fastest capability
gains recorded on any benchmark in the report.

The same report also introduces RE-Bench, which tests AI agents on more
open-ended engineering tasks across a range of time budgets. It finds
that within a short time budget (around two hours), top AI systems
score roughly four times higher than human experts on these tasks; but
as the time budget increases, human performance overtakes AI, with
humans outscoring AI systems roughly two to one at a 32-hour budget.
The report's authors read this as evidence that AI currently excels at
short, well-bounded engineering problems, while sustained, longer-horizon
engineering work -- the kind that involves accumulating context,
managing trade-offs over time, and adapting a plan as understanding
deepens -- still favors human engineers.

Read alongside real-world field studies such as METR's 2025 finding
that AI tools slowed experienced developers down on long-familiar,
complex codebases (see ai_coding_limitations.md), the benchmark trend
and the field-study result are not necessarily contradictory: both are
consistent with AI capability advancing fastest on short, bounded,
well-specified tasks, while the messier, longer-horizon parts of real
software engineering work remain comparatively harder for AI systems to
handle as well as experienced humans.

RELEVANCE: Provides balanced evidence for how software engineering roles
may change: rapid AI progress on bounded/benchmarked tasks (pro-AI
capability), paired with an explicit finding that AI still lags humans
on sustained, long-horizon engineering work (AI-limitations), useful for
either side depending on which timescale of the debate topic is being
argued.
