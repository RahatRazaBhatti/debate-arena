TOPIC: Software developer employment trends, AI and labor markets

CONTENT:
The BLS projects overall employment of software developers, quality
assurance analysts, and testers to grow 15% from 2024 to 2034, much
faster than the 3% average projected growth across all occupations,
adding an estimated 267,000+ jobs and roughly 129,200 openings per year
on average (counting both growth and replacement of workers who leave
the field). The BLS attributes continued strong demand partly to
expanding software needs in AI, IoT, robotics, and automation, as well
as growing investment in computer security.

Notably, the BLS draws a sharp distinction between "software
developers" as a broad occupational category (design, architecture,
system-level engineering) and the narrower "computer programmers"
category (translating detailed specifications into code line by line).
Employment of computer programmers specifically is projected to
DECLINE 6% over the same 2024-2034 period, with the BLS explicitly
noting that some of the more routine, lower-skill programming tasks
are expected to shift toward software developers and AI-assisted
tooling rather than remaining a standalone entry point into the field.

This divergence -- growth for developers, decline for narrower
"programmer" roles -- suggests AI and automation may be reshaping which
tasks constitute programming work rather than eliminating the broader
software engineering profession outright.

RELEVANCE: Provides direct employment/labor-market evidence relevant to
both sides: growth for the broader software developer occupation
(anti-replacement), alongside a real decline in the narrowest,
most automatable coding-only roles (pro-automation-impact evidence).
