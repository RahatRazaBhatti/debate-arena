SOURCE: arXiv (industry case study)
TITLE: Experience with GitHub Copilot for Developer Productivity at Zoominfo
AUTHOR/ORGANIZATION: ZoomInfo Technologies engineering research team
DATE: January 2025
URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13282v1
TOPIC: AI reducing repetitive programming work, developer efficiency, AI coding assistants

CONTENT:
ZoomInfo ran a two-week controlled trial of GitHub Copilot across 126
engineers, then surveyed 72 of them (a 57% response rate) about their
experience. Copilot showed consistent suggestion-acceptance rates across
different programming languages: roughly 33% of suggestions and 20% of
full lines were accepted, which the authors describe as evidence of
reliable, repeatable usefulness rather than a novelty effect confined to
one language or team.

The most consistent theme in the survey responses was time saved on
repetitive, low-judgment work: developers specifically called out
boilerplate code, unit tests, meaningful variable naming, and
documentation and comments as the areas where Copilot saved the most
time. The average self-reported productivity improvement was 7.6 out of
10. On code quality, the majority of participants said Copilot's
suggestions aligned well with their team's existing coding standards,
and no participants reported a decline in code quality across their
team's pull requests during the trial.

The report is explicit that this was a internally-run engineering trial
rather than an independent academic study, and that the security
implications of AI-suggested code required a deliberate rollout with
security training, which the team built into its phased adoption plan.

RELEVANCE: Supports the "AI reducing repetitive programming work" and
"developer efficiency" pro-AI categories with a real company's internal,
methodologically transparent trial data (acceptance rates, self-reported
time savings, and a note on where the gains concentrated).
