TITLE: Accelerate State of DevOps Report 2024
AUTHOR/ORGANIZATION: DORA / Google Cloud
DATE: 2024
URL: https://dora.dev/research/2024/dora-report/
TOPIC: Developer efficiency, AI-assisted software engineering, empirical studies on AI-assisted development

CONTENT:
DORA's annual State of DevOps report is one of the longest-running,
most widely cited large-scale surveys of software engineering practice,
and its 2024 edition dedicated substantial coverage to measuring AI's
real-world impact on developers and delivery. The topline finding was
explicitly two-sided: a 25% increase in a team's AI adoption was
associated with a roughly 2.1% increase in individual productivity and a
roughly 2.6% increase in job satisfaction, alongside measurable
improvements in code quality, code review speed, reduced code
complexity, less technical debt, and better documentation. 75.9% of
surveyed developers reported using AI daily for tasks like writing code,
summarizing information, debugging, and testing, and 75% of respondents
overall reported some productivity gain from AI.

At the same time, the same 25% increase in AI adoption was associated
with roughly a 1.5% decrease in software delivery throughput and a 7.2%
decrease in delivery stability at the team/system level - meaning
individual developers felt and were faster, but the larger software
delivery pipeline did not automatically get faster or more stable as a
result. DORA's researchers attribute part of this gap to AI making it
easier to produce larger code changes, and larger changesets carry more
risk of introducing defects, independent of whether the code itself is
"good." Roughly 39% of respondents reported some distrust of AI-generated
code, which the report ties to the continued need for review and human
oversight even as adoption grows.

RELEVANCE: A large-scale, methodologically rigorous, genuinely two-sided
data point: real evidence of individual developer efficiency gains from
AI (pro-AI), paired with an honest finding that those individual gains do
not automatically translate into faster or more stable software delivery
at the organizational level (a limitation). Useful for either side
depending on which level of the debate topic - the individual developer
role, versus the overall software delivery system - is being argued.
