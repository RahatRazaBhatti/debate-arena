SOURCE: METR (Model Evaluation and Threat Research)
TITLE: Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity
AUTHOR/ORGANIZATION: METR (Becker, Rush, Barnes, Rein)
DATE: July 10, 2025
URL: https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/
TOPIC: AI coding tools, developer productivity, limitations of AI assistance

CONTENT:
METR, an independent nonprofit AI evaluation organization, ran a
randomized controlled trial with 16 experienced open-source developers
completing 246 real tasks in mature codebases they knew well (an
average of five years of prior experience with those specific
projects). Each task was randomly assigned to either allow or forbid
the use of contemporary AI coding tools (primarily Cursor Pro with
Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet, the frontier tools available in early 2025).

The result ran counter to expectations: when developers were allowed to
use AI tools, they took about 19% longer to complete their tasks than
when working without AI. This is especially notable because, before the
study, developers forecast that AI would speed them up by roughly 24%,
and even after finishing the tasks and experiencing the actual
slowdown, they still estimated they had been about 20% faster with AI.
Researchers noted a large gap between developers' subjective sense of
productivity and objectively measured completion time.

The authors suggest several contributing factors: developers spent
meaningful time reviewing, correcting, and cleaning up AI-suggested
code that was often "directionally correct" but not quite right for
the specific, highly familiar codebase; and the benefit of AI
assistance may be smaller for engineers who already have deep,
tacit knowledge of a large, mature project, compared to newer or less
familiar codebases where other studies have found larger gains.

RELEVANCE: Supports the case that AI coding tools have real limitations
in complex, real-world engineering work, and that developers'
perception of AI's benefit can diverge sharply from measured reality
(anti-replacement / AI-limitations evidence).
