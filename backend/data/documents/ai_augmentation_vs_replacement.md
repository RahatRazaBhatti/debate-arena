SOURCE: Gartner, Inc.; McKinsey Global Institute
TITLE: AI Will Not Replace Software Engineers (and May, in Fact, Require More); Gartner Says Generative AI Will Require 80% of Engineering Workforce to Upskill Through 2027
AUTHOR/ORGANIZATION: Gartner, Inc. (Philip Walsh et al.); McKinsey Global Institute
DATE: August 2024 (Gartner report); October 2024 (Gartner upskilling forecast); ongoing McKinsey estimates
URL: https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-10-03-gartner-says-generative-ai-will-require-80-percent-of-engineering-workforce-to-upskill-through-2027
TOPIC: AI augmenting rather than replacing developers, changing software engineering roles

CONTENT:
Gartner's research explicitly rejects the idea that generative AI will
simply eliminate the software engineering profession. Analyst Philip
Walsh states that while AI will transform the role of software
engineers, human expertise and creativity remain essential to
delivering complex, innovative software. Gartner forecasts that through
2027, generative AI will create new roles in software engineering and
operations, and will require roughly 80% of the existing engineering
workforce to upskill rather than be displaced.

Gartner describes this unfolding in three phases: in the near term, AI
tools deliver modest productivity gains mainly for senior developers at
organizations with mature engineering practices; in the medium term, AI
agents take on more fully automated tasks, pushing engineers toward
directing and validating AI output rather than writing every line by
hand; and in the longer term, Gartner expects rising demand for a new
"AI engineer" role focused on building AI-empowered software, meaning
organizations will need MORE skilled engineers overall, not fewer, to
meet that demand.

Separately, the McKinsey Global Institute's broader (economy-wide, not
software-specific) estimate is that generative AI could eventually
automate activities accounting for up to roughly 30% of current work
hours across the economy -- a meaningful share of routine tasks, but
one that assumes most jobs are transformed rather than eliminated
outright, since most occupations combine automatable and
non-automatable tasks.

RELEVANCE: Directly supports the "augmentation, not replacement" framing
requested by the debate: both organizations argue AI reshapes the
software engineering role and demands new skills, rather than making
the profession obsolete (balanced / augmentation evidence).
