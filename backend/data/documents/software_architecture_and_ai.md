TITLE: Gartner Survey Finds 77% of Engineering Leaders Identify AI Integration in Apps as a Major Challenge; related Gartner software engineering forecasts
AUTHOR/ORGANIZATION: Gartner, Inc. (Jim Scheibmeir and other Gartner analysts)
DATE: May 22, 2025 (survey); ongoing Gartner forecasts through 2026
URL: https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-05-22-gartner-survey-finds-77-percent-of-engineering-leaders-identify-ai-integration-in-apps-as-a-major-challenge
TOPIC: Software architecture, AI limitations in complex systems, human oversight

CONTENT:
A Gartner survey of 400 U.S. and U.K. software engineering and
application-development leaders (conducted October-December 2024) found
that 77% consider building AI capabilities into applications a
significant or moderate pain point, and 71% said the same about using
AI tools to augment engineering workflows more broadly. Gartner
analysts note that organizations still need to carefully review the
output of AI-augmented development tools, which requires engineers with
strong foundational skills rather than replacing that expertise.

Gartner separately forecasts that by the end of 2026, roughly 40% of
enterprise applications will run task-specific AI agents in production,
up from under 5% in 2025 -- but frames this as applying mainly to
clean, well-specified, bounded work. The firm's analysis is that the
remaining majority of engineering work, especially anything touching
security-sensitive logic, customer-facing behavior, or overall system
architecture, still requires human gatekeeping through code review,
test coverage, and release controls. Their framing of the engineer's
evolving role is a shift from writing every line of code by hand toward
specifying constraints, validating AI-generated output, and owning
production outcomes.

RELEVANCE: Supports the case that AI is not yet reliable enough to be
trusted with architecture-level decisions or safety/security-critical
systems without human review (AI-limitations evidence), while
acknowledging AI is rapidly taking over narrower, well-defined tasks.
